INTRODUCTION — REAL MONEY ALLOCATION (PREAMBLE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In this part of the study, you will participate in a research activity in which you will decide how to distribute real money between two Pakistani citizens. This is not a survey asking your opinions. Instead, you'll be making decisions about how to distribute significant sums of real money to real people.

We are collaborating with the Centre for Research and Economic Development (CRED) to facilitate these payments. The recipients will be randomly selected from a list of over two million individuals from Punjab. This list, managed by CRED, contains detailed information about their backgrounds and financial situations. The average pre-tax income within this group is 45,000 rupees per month.

Please click the NEXT button below to proceed to the next screen.

PART A — THE DECISION PROBLEM (INSTRUCTIONS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The decision problem

This part of the study consists of two phases, each comprising 10 decision problems. In each problem, you will allocate rupees between two Pakistani citizens, labeled X and Y. Each decision will be represented by a graph (as in the example on the next screen). For each decision problem, you will be asked to select a point along a line that represents the possible divisions of money between citizens X and Y. The amount that you give to citizen X will be represented on the horizontal axis, and the amount that you give to citizen Y on the vertical axis.

We note that thousands of individuals from various countries—including groups of law students, medical students, physicians, and high-level civil servants—have participated in similar research activities. This research activity helps us understand how people make decisions about sharing resources, building on what we have learned from earlier research studies.

Please click the NEXT button below to proceed to the next screen.

EXAMPLES OF DECISION PROBLEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each decision problem begins with the computer randomly selecting a downward-sloping line from a set of lines that intersect one axis—X or Y—below 500 rupees, and the other between 500 and 1000 rupees. Below are examples of possible lines, varying in slope: steep (left), intermediate (middle), and flat (right).

[Graph examples referenced in survey UI]

Using your mouse, you may select any point (X, Y) along the line. This point determines the amount of money allocated to the two citizens, X and Y. Importantly, when the line is steep, you can potentially allocate more money to citizen Y; when the line is flat, you can potentially allocate more to citizen X. The slope of the line shows the rate at which money must be taken from one citizen to increase the payment to the other: A steeper slope means that increasing Y's payment requires only a smaller reduction in X's payment. Conversely, a flatter slope means that increasing X's payment requires only a smaller reduction in Y's payment.

Each decision problem is independent—your previous choices do not influence the lines selected in problems that follow. For each decision, you may choose any (X, Y) pair on the line, thereby determining the allocation of money between citizens X and Y. Keep in mind that because the line is downward sloping, increasing the payout to citizen X necessarily reduces the amount allocated to citizen Y, and vice versa.

PHASES, PAYMENTS, AND RECIPIENT POOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are now about to begin the activity. It consists of two phases. Each consists of 10 independent decision problems as described above, in which you will allocate money between two citizens, X and Y. At the end of each phase, one round will be randomly selected, and the amounts that you chose in that round will actually be paid out. In the selected decision problem, one randomly chosen Pakistani citizen will receive the amount allocated to person X, and another randomly chosen Pakistani citizen will receive the amount allocated to person Y.

The two phases differ in the types of Pakistani citizens X and Y that will be chosen as recipients. In Phase 1, both individuals are drawn from the general sample of Pakistani citizens, which has an average pre-tax monthly income of 45,000 rupees. In Phase 2, person Y is randomly selected from those with pre-tax monthly income above 65,000 rupees, while person X is selected from those with a pre-tax monthly income below 25,000 rupees.

There are no right or wrong choices. But please think carefully about your allocations, since real money will be given to two people based on each selected decision.

PART 2 — LEGAL CASES (VIGNETTES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The next (second) part of the study examines how you make decisions in legal cases. We have designed four cases that involve high conflicts among fundamental societal values that can arise in the context of legal disputes. To understand how you would rule on these cases if they were presented in your court, this section includes one open-ended question followed by multiple-choice questions.

You may skip the open-ended question, but the multiple-choice questions must be answered in order to proceed. That said, your thoughtful responses to the open-ended question are extremely valuable to our research, and we would greatly appreciate your insights.

CASE 1 — THE SHANGLA WOOD DISPUTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A businessman in Lahore hired a builder to construct a new house. The businessman moved to the city from a village in the Shangla District when he was young and still feels a strong connection to his childhood home. He wants his new house to reflect his roots, so he insisted that it be made using wood from his village. The contract with the builder states that all wood must come from the Shangla District. If any other wood is used, it must be replaced with the correct wood. However, due to a mistake, the builder used imported wood instead. This mistake went unnoticed until the house was finished because the two types of wood look the same. The only way to fix this error is for the house to be torn down and rebuilt with the correct wood. The businessman has sued the builder to get money for the cost of tearing down and rebuilding the house. But because the woods are the same, the mistake did not change the house's market value. The builder admits he breached the contract but argues that he should not pay any damages because the mistake has not reduced the market value of the house.

What remedy should the court order?

If you had to choose from the following menu of remedies, which would you choose? Please check the box next to the remedy that you would award.

- The total cost to knock down the current house and build a new one with wood from the Shangla District.
- The difference in value between the house as it is and what it would have been worth if made with Shangla wood, which is zero.
- The court's best guess of how much money it would take to compensate the businessman for his disappointment at not having a house made with Shangla wood.
- The money saved by the builder for using imported wood instead of Shangla wood.

Leave aside what you think is the best solution. How appropriate do you think it would be to settle the case in these ways? Please check the box below the answer you choose.

CASE 2 — THE CEMENT FACTORY DISPUTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A new cement factory has opened near limestone hills and sandy riverbeds to take advantage of the nearby materials needed for cement. The factory cost 3 billion PKR to build. The factory is very productive, making 50,000 tons of cement each year, and it earns profits of 150 million PKR a year. It employs over 1000 people. However, even though the factory uses the best available dust control measures, the dust from the factory has made some nearby tomato farms unusable. These farms have been family-run for many generations, but together they only earn 3 million PRK a year and employ 30 people. The farm owners have sued the cement factory, claiming that the dust constitutes a nuisance.

How should the court decide the case? If you had to choose from the following menu of decisions, which would you choose? Please check the box next to the remedy that you would award.

- The cement factory does not constitute a nuisance.
- The cement factory constitutes a nuisance, and the remedy is damages for the economic loss that the dust imposes on the farm-owners.
- The cement factory constitutes a nuisance, and the remedy is an injunction halting the factory's operation.

Leaving aside which you think is the best remedy, how appropriate do you think it would be to resolve the case by ruling in each of these ways? Please check the box below the answer you choose.

For each option below, scale: Very appropriate / Appropriate / Neither appropriate nor inappropriate / Inappropriate / Very inappropriate

- The cement factory does not constitute a nuisance.
- The cement factory constitutes a nuisance, and the remedy is damages for the economic loss that the dust imposes on the farm-owners.

CASE 4 — PROPORTIONALITY IN TRESPASS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A developer built a new office tower on land he bought. Before building, he hired a surveyor to check the land boundaries. The surveyor was reputable and took reasonable care, but he made a small mistake, and if the tower was built as planned, it would extend 10 cm into a nearby parking lot owned by a shopping mall. The developer found out about the mistake after getting the building plans approved. Changing the building now would take 6 months and cost an extra 10%. Since the slight overlap didn't affect the mall or parking lot, the developer decided to keep building. Six months later, after the offices were filled with businesses, the mall owner found out about the overlap and sued the developer. The mall owner is asking the court to enjoin the developer's trespass. The only way to eliminate the trespass would be to tear down the office tower and move it 10 cm, which would cost 20 million PKR for the developer and disrupt the office workers.

How should the court decide the case?

Leaving aside which you think is the best remedy, how appropriate do you think it would be to resolve the case by ruling in each of these ways? Please check the box below the answer you choose.

For each option below, scale: Very appropriate / Appropriate / Neither appropriate nor inappropriate / Inappropriate / Very inappropriate

- The developer did not commit a trespass.
- The developer committed a trespass, and the remedy is the reduction in value of the mall and its parking lot, which is zero.
- The developer committed a trespass, and the remedy is an injunction to tear down the office tower and move it to eliminate the encroachment.
- The developer committed a trespass, and the remedy is disgorgement of the construction costs saved by the decision to build rather than redesigning the building and getting renewed planning permission.

FRAMING — DISTRIBUTION PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Some argue that a society's primary goal should be to ensure equal distribution of income and opportunity—even if it comes at the expense of maximizing overall wealth. Others contend that it is better to allocate resources in a way that generates the greatest total wealth, even if that wealth is distributed unequally. Imagine that you were free to distribute resources in your society across people. Which of the principles below do you think are important?

Scale for each (1 = Least important … 10 = Most important):

- Distributing resources equally
- Giving the most resources to the people or groups most in need
- Giving the most resources to the people or groups who would use them in the most productive ways

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
